# Frequently Asked Questions

## Getting Started

**How much does a LowRider v4 cost?**  
The average total build cost is about $650 if you buy the full kit from the shop. The only thing
not included is a ~$120 router and a table. If you print your own parts you can lower that to just
over $400. Most people get cutting for under $800 USD.

**What can it cut?**  
Foams, wood, and plastic are easy. Aluminum and other soft metals are no problem for an experienced
user. A few users have even machined steel.

**How big can I build it?**  
Technically any size, but the most common builds are full sheet (4'×8'), half sheet (4'×4'), or
quarter sheet (2'×4'). Larger builds cut more slowly. Use the
[Size Calculator](/lowrider/build/calculator) for your exact dimensions.

**What size should I build?**  
Width (X axis, the Beam) should always be the shorter dimension. Beyond that, build what fits your
space and your most common material size. Most people start with full sheet.

## Parts & Printing

**What filament should I use?**  
PLA is recommended for ultimate rigidity. PET-GF/CF and PC-GF/CF are good alternatives if you need
heat resistance.

**Do I need to print everything myself?**  
No. V1 Engineering sells [printed part sets](https://www.v1e.com/products/lowrider-v4-printed-parts-with-aluminum-xz-plates)
with the aluminum XZ plates included.

**Where do I get rails?**  
Self-source locally. In the US: 1" EMT conduit (29.5 mm OD) from any hardware store.
Other countries typically use 30 mm or 32 mm OD tube. See [Planning](./build/planning) for full specs.

**What control board should I use?**  
The [Jackpot CNC Controller](/lowrider/controllers/jackpot) is purpose-built for V1 machines and
is strongly recommended for new builds.

## Assembly

**How long does the build take?**  
See what [actual users report on the forum](https://forum.v1e.com/t/lr4-how-many-hours-to-build-lr4/45641/1).

**Do I need power tools to assemble it?**  
Hand tools only — power tools cause more problems than they solve. Snug is enough; nothing needs
to be cranked down.

**My Core tension bolts — how tight?**  
Leave them loose initially. You want the Core seated without rattling. Loose is better than too
tight; it is simple to add tension later but difficult to replace a broken core. If the core
tightens and loosens as it crosses over braces, it is too tight.

**Belt tension — how tight?**  
Target about 7 lbs (3.2 kg) of tension. It will **not** pluck like a guitar string. Always start
loose. The parts will break long before the belt does.

## Calibration

**My machine isn't cutting square — what do I do?**  
Mark four corner dots, measure both diagonals, and compare. If the difference is over 2 mm, adjust
the Y pull-off in firmware. See [Calibration](./build/calibration) for step-by-step instructions.

**My Z axis isn't level — one side cuts higher than the other.**  
Use the Z leveling procedure to probe both sides and adjust the pull-off distance for the
more-negative side. See [Calibration](./build/calibration).

**My cuts are coming out several millimeters short — why?**  
GT2 belts vary slightly in pitch. Run the belt calibration procedure to measure your actual belt
pitch and adjust steps/mm. See [Calibration](./build/calibration).

## Troubleshooting

**The machine hits a hard stop before the endstop triggers.**  
The Y endstop screws are too far in. Adjust them outward so the endstop triggers just before the
machine reaches the physical stop.

**The endstop lever is getting popped or bent.**  
The Y stop screw is too far out (or missing). Set it so the endstop triggers about 4 mm before
the end of travel.

**The beam sits crooked / heels and toes don't match.**  
Check back wheel alignment on both YZ plates — outer faces should be flush with the wheel mounts.
The lower strut plate screws at each end control the heel-toe distance.

**The lower X rail is touching the metal XZ plate.**  
This causes driving or lifting issues. The lower X rail should not contact the XZ plate. Check
your rail lengths against the calculator values.

**I can't get my Z stubs perpendicular to the leadscrew.**  
Z stubs that tilt or twist cause Z binding. Make sure M5 nuts are fully seated and snug both
screws evenly.
