# Initial Calibration

## Belt Calibration

The GT2 belts actually vary in pitch slightly. On smaller machines you might not notice; on a full
sheet machine it can show up. Luckily it is a simple test to do on initial setup or if you change
your belts. Over 2400 mm it can vary up to ±6 mm.

Mark a spot near the closest position on your table, move a known distance, and mark another point
at the far end. Then adjust steps per millimeter to match your belts. Only need to do this on one
axis — your longest, for the most accuracy — and it applies to both X and Y if you're using the
same belts.

The math: `(Distance told / Distance actually moved) × Steps per mm = new Steps per mm`

Example: `(2400/2394.2) × 50 = 50.121 steps`

On Jackpot boards: go into `config.yaml` and change both X and Y `steps_per_mm: 50.000` to the
new value. Use the save macro and reboot. Test the new settings.

I prefer a [V-Bit](https://www.v1e.com/products/1-8-45-degree-v-bit) in the router, but you can
use the pen mount to mark a small dot.

![Belt calibration tape](/img/lowrider/v4/belt1.jpg)

* Use tape at each end so you can be sure you're using the correct mark.
* Home X and Y. Use the Z axis to mark a small dot near your table edge.
* Move your machine to the far end, keep track of how far you asked it to move, and make another mark.
* Move the CNC out of the way.
* Measure these marks to the best of your ability.

---

![Belt calibration dot](/img/lowrider/v4/belt2.jpg)

* Here is what the dot looks like from a V-bit.
* Get a helper to hold one end while you read the other.
* Verify your new settings — usually one try is all it takes, and this should not change.

## Squaring

To square the machine, mark 4 points and measure the diagonal. This is how we set the Y axis to
square the machine relative to the rail every time you home. You will likely need to repeat this
a couple of times to get under 1 mm difference.

You can use a [V-Bit](https://www.v1e.com/products/1-8-45-degree-v-bit) to pop a tiny hole into
tape, or use the pen mount. Here is sample G-code you can edit for your build size:

```
$H ;Home all three axes
G1 X30 Y30 F6000 ;Move to starting corner
G1 Z-98 F6000 ;Move down to mark first hole
G1 Z-78 F600 ;Lift Z axis up before it moves
G1 X1190 F6000 ;Move to right side
G1 Z-98 F600
G1 Z-78
G1 Y2400 F6000 ;Move to back right
G1 Z-98 F600
G1 Z-78
G1 X30 F6000 ;Move to back left
G1 Z-98 F600
G1 Z-78
G1 Y1200 Z0 F6000 ;Move out of the way so you can measure
```

---

![Squaring corner 1](/img/lowrider/v4/sq1.jpg)

* Use tape to mark, so you can change it out for each test.
* Home X and Y. Use the Z axis to mark a dot at 0,0.

---

![Squaring X extreme](/img/lowrider/v4/sq2.jpg)

* Drive the machine to your X axis furthest point and mark the dot.

---

![Squaring Y extreme](/img/lowrider/v4/sq3.jpg)

* Drive to the Y extreme and repeat for the back two corners.

---

![Squaring diagonal](/img/lowrider/v4/sq4.jpg)

* Measure the diagonal to the best of your ability.

---

![Squaring other diagonal](/img/lowrider/v4/sq4.jpg)

* Measure the other diagonal. Note the longer one and subtract to find the difference. Under 1 mm
  is very good.

---

If you have a difference, you have a parallelogram instead of a rectangle. Corrections should be
made if you are over 2 mm of difference, depending on use case. A good first guess for your offset
is half the difference. The corner nearest the endstop associated with the longer diagonal needs to
move further from the Y hard stop. This is done in firmware.

Coarse adjustments can be made with the Y-axis endstop screws. Too far in: hits the hard stops
before the switches. Too far out: risk of popping the endstop levers.

<details>
<summary>If you prefer to use a pen, use Jamie's Automated "Squareness Marks"</summary>

You can also automate this squaring process with Jamie's
["Squareness Marks"](https://vector76.github.io/gcode_tpgen/) G-code generator.

- Home the X and Y axis.
- Start with `G92 X0 Y0 Z0` — where the tip is will be 0,0,0. Start the tip just above the paper.
- **Z level for tip-down** — Make this number more negative if some marks are not hitting the paper.
  Use as light a touch as possible.
- **Z level for tip-up** — Go as high as needed so the tip does not drag.
- **Feedrate** — The default is safe.
- **Extents** — Set the dimensions of your table. The further apart the marks, the more accurate.
- Note: the V-bit method is a bit more accurate because a pen can deflect slightly.

## Z Leveling

Z Leveling makes sure the Beam is level with the work surface. The Z endstops are adjustable so
there can be slight differences in each side's homing location. Getting under 0.1 mm difference is
pretty easy with this method.

You will need to redo this after rebuilding your beam with the strut plates, but it will likely be
very close. When the beam gets rebuilt you can surface your entire work surface.

Take a few measurements at each end in the same place, average each side's position, then adjust
the Z offset for the more-negative side (more negative = endstop triggered higher). After
adjustments, save config, reboot, and test again.

G-code to automate this on the Jackpot board — run several times at each position and average:

```
$HZ
G10 L2 P0 Z0 ;set Z to zero
G1 Z-70 F800 ;move down fast
G38.2 Z-100 F60 ;probe slow
```

The advanced way in FluidNC: run [Jason's G-code script](https://forum.v1e.com/t/gcode-variables-and-loops/41588/17?u=vicious1).

---

![Z leveling touch plate](/img/lowrider/v4/levelz1.jpg)

* Z leveling is easier if you have a touch plate. You can also use any block or piece of paper as a
  feeler gauge.
* Move 30–50 mm in from each edge when testing.
* Tape the touch plate down to increase accuracy.
* Home the Z axis.
* Set Z to 0: in FluidNC `G10 L2 P0 Z0`; in Marlin `G92 Z0`.
* Probe slowly to the touch plate. In FluidNC: `G38.2 Z-100 F60`; in Marlin: `G38.2 Z0`.
* Note the current Z position. Do this at least 3 times on each side and average.

---

![Z leveling FluidNC position](/img/lowrider/v4/levelz3.png)

* In FluidNC this is your current Z position.
* In Marlin this shows on your LCD screen or G-code sender.

---

![Z leveling repeat other side](/img/lowrider/v4/levelz2.jpg)

* Repeat for the other side.
* Always hold or tape down your touch plate for best accuracy.
* Again, repeat 3 times and average.

---

Take the difference and adjust the more-negative side. If Z0 = –97 and Z1 = –98, the difference is
1 mm. Add 1 mm to the pull-off setting for Z1. Default in FluidNC is 4 mm pull-off, so change to
5 mm. We need at least 4 mm for the trigger to reset — never make it smaller. Save, power cycle,
then retest each side once to verify (under 0.5 mm for common use).

For Marlin: adjust with `M666 Z1` (moves right side down) or `M666 Z-1` (moves left side down),
then `M500` to save. Power cycle and retest.
