# Planning Your LowRider v4

Before ordering parts, a few sizing decisions need to be made. The most important is your cutting
area — everything else (rail lengths, belts, table size) flows from that one number. Use the
[Size Calculator](./calculator) to get all the exact dimensions once you've decided.

## Choosing Your Size

A typical build targets one of three material sizes:

| Build Size | Work Area (approx.) |
|------------|---------------------|
| Full sheet | 4' × 8' (1200 × 2440 mm) |
| Half sheet | 4' × 4' (1200 × 1200 mm) |
| Quarter sheet | 2' × 4' (600 × 1200 mm) |

A few things to keep in mind:

* **Width (X axis, "Beam") should always be the shorter axis.** A narrower beam is more rigid.
* Larger builds cut more slowly — a full sheet machine is great for sheet goods but not ideal if
  you mainly do small detailed work.
* Standard Z cutting depth is 50 mm; a drop table gives all 100 mm.
* The machine is most rigid near the table surface — rigidity increases as your cut progresses
  downward.
* Two Y rails would be extremely difficult to align. The single-rail design keeps things constrained
  and easy to use.

## Rails

You will need to self-source the rails. This is one of the few parts not available from the V1 shop.

You are looking for **29.5 mm, 30 mm, or 32 mm OD steel tubes**. In the US and parts of Canada this
is 1" EMT Conduit (29.5 mm) from a hardware store (not "rigid" conduit). Other countries typically
use 30 mm or 32 mm OD.

If you want a different rail material, look for:

* OD ±0.2 mm of 29.5 mm, 30 mm, or 32 mm.
* At least 1.3 mm wall thickness.
* Steel, stainless steel, or DOM steel — aluminum and carbon fiber will not work.
* Super thick walls, solid rods, and filled tubes add mass and cost with little rigidity gain.

If your country does not use metal conduit: metal supply companies, closet rods, or boat suppliers
tend to have stainless tubes.
