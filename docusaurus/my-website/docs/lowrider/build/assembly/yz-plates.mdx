# YZ Plate Assemblies

Build two of these — one for each side (Min and Max). They are mirror images of each other.

![YZ Plate finished](/img/lowrider/v4/fullYZplate.jpg)

* This is what the finished assembly will look like.

---

![YZ Plate label wires](/img/lowrider/v4/label.jpg)

* Label all wires before you run them. Tight to the wire, not the plugs.
* Typically, facing the machine: left side = Y0/Z0, right side = Y1/Z1.

---

![YZ endstop 1](/img/lowrider/v4/yze1.jpg)

* Use M2.5 screws to secure the crimped or soldered endstop to the Z\_Stop\_Min/Max part.

---

![YZ endstop 2](/img/lowrider/v4/yze2.jpg)

* Notice the lever faces away from the flat side.

---

![YZ endstop 2a](/img/lowrider/v4/yze2a.jpg)

* The nut gets pressed in from the YZ plate face.
* If the nut is not tight, use a flat head screwdriver to hold it while tightening.

---

![YZ endstop 3](/img/lowrider/v4/yze3.jpg)

* Run the wire through the channel.

---

![YZ endstop 4](/img/lowrider/v4/yze4.jpg)

* Lightly secure the Z stop part with an M5 screw and nut.
* Start in the low position — this gets adjusted later.

---

![YZ endstop 5](/img/lowrider/v4/yze5.jpg)

* Run the endstop wire all the way out of the bottom of the YZ plate.

---

![YZ endstop 6](/img/lowrider/v4/yze6.jpg)

* Insert both M5 idler nuts into place.

---

![YZ endstop 7](/img/lowrider/v4/yze7.jpg)

* Add the M5 screws to hold the nuts until later.
* A flat-head screwdriver can help push them into place.

---

![YZ stepper 1](/img/lowrider/v4/yzs1.jpg)

* Use the alignment gauge on the top of the YZ plate to align the pulley.
* Secure with set/grub screw on the flat face first, then the other.
* Stepper is on the chamfered/endstop side.

---

![YZ stepper 2](/img/lowrider/v4/yzs2.jpg)

* Label the wire Y0 or Y1.
* Feed the wire through the channel on the larger side of the hole.
* Mount the stepper with wires nearest the channel.

---

![YZ stepper 3](/img/lowrider/v4/yzs3.jpg)

* Use two M3 screws to hold the stepper lightly — the other two come later.

---

![YZ front wheel 1](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw1.jpg)

* Build the front wheel — both sides are similar. Bolt heads face outside the machine or down.
* Snug up the large bolts without crushing the part.

---

![YZ front wheel 2](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw2.jpg)

* Two M3 screws secure the front wheel and the rest of the Y stepper.

---

![YZ front wheel 3](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw3.jpg)

* Snug all 4 stepper screws.
* Ensure the outer faces of the YZ plate and wheel are flush. Lay on a flat table with wheels
  hanging off the edge to align before snugging.

---

![YZ belt 1](/img/lowrider/v4/yzb1.jpg)

* Load the Y axis belt around the pulley — tweezers or a small screwdriver can help guide it.
* Cut to length per the [calculator](../calculator).
* You can remove the M5 screws to make this easier.

---

![YZ belt 2](/img/lowrider/v4/yzb2.jpg)

* Add the idlers and M5 screws back.
* Seat the screws but do not crush the plastic or bearing.
* A drop of light oil inside these bearings can extend their life.

---

![YZ endstop 8](/img/lowrider/v4/yze8.jpg)

* Wire labeled as Y0 or Y1.
* Run wires through the first channel.

---

![YZ endstop 9](/img/lowrider/v4/yze9.jpg)

* Use M2.5 screws to secure the endstop with the arm facing out.
* 99% of builds use this endstop homing to Ymin. The Ymax location is only for those who want to
  home to Ymax (not recommended for beginners, requires config changes).

:::warning
From this point on, be cautious not to snag the little lever on anything. You can hang the wheels
and lever off the edge of the table if needed.
:::

---

![YZ back wheel 4](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw4.jpg)

* Run one wire at a time through the keeper — this makes it very easy.
* Run small wires through first, then stepper wires to hold them all in.
* If having a hard time, push the wires in with your pulley allen key or a flat head — do not pry
  the tab.
* If stepper wires have any sort of wire covering, you will need to remove it.

---

![YZ back wheel 5](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw5.jpg)

* All wires should be to the back of the plate.
* Feed them one at a time out of the slot in the back.

---

![YZ back wheel 6](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw6.jpg)

* When all wires are through, bundle them.

---

![YZ Z coupler 1](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz1.jpg)

* Use the guide on the top of the YZ plate to align the coupler — use the small furthest line.
* Stepper is on the endstop side of the plate.
* Tighten the set/grub screw on the flat first, then the other.

---

![YZ Z coupler 2](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz2.jpg)

* Run wire out the back channel — notice which side of the hole it goes in from.
* Jiggle other wires if needed to let it feed through. Bundle for safety.

---

![YZ Z coupler 2a](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz2a.jpg)

* M3 screws to secure the stepper — start all 4 first, then final snug.

---

![YZ Z rail prep](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz3.jpg)

* Use the sharp edge of the linear rail to chisel the rail bed smooth.
* Any print artifacts, blobs, or rough spots should come right off.
* This simple step makes both rails sit better and prevents twists.

---

![YZ Z rail attach](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz4.jpg)

* Y endstop hanging off the table to protect the small lever.
* Loosely add all M3 screws to the linear rails, getting heads just under the rail surface.
* Watch for off-center M3 screw heads — they can make the next step harder.

---

![YZ Z rail XZ plate](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz5.jpg)

* Add XZ plate using M3 screws to the bearing blocks. Snug.
* XZ max has the large hole.
* XZ min has the slot at the top (shown in this picture).

---

![YZ Z rail final](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz6.jpg)

* Snug all rail screws.
* Move the XZ plate the full range of motion for each screw to assure no binding.
* After rails are tight, loosen each bearing block and re-snug — this usually gains a little more
  smooth action.

---

![YZ Z leadscrew](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz6a.jpg)

* Add the T8 leadscrew to the coupler. Make sure it is fully seated touching the stepper shaft.
* Snug both grub screws.
* Lubricate the leadscrew with a light lubricant. [Shop](https://www.v1e.com/products/super-lube-silicone-lubricating-grease-with-syncolon-ptfe)

---

![YZ Z nut](/img/lowrider/v4/yzz7.jpg)

* Add the brass nut to the Z\_Nut — 1–2 M3 screws is all that's needed.
* This can be a snug fit; use pliers to press if needed.
* Thread this onto the leadscrew, brass face down.

---

![YZ Z stub 4](/img/lowrider/v4/yzs4.jpg)

* Z stubs get 2 hidden nuts and are secured to the XZ plates with M5 screws.

---

![YZ Z stub 5](/img/lowrider/v4/yzs5.jpg)

* The hidden M5 nuts get pressed in and fully seated.

---

![YZ Z stub 6](/img/lowrider/v4/yzs6.jpg)

* If trouble seating the nuts: press them in with a flat head (loose print) or thread the M5
  through the XZ plate and use it to pull them into place (tight print).

---

![YZ Z stub 7](/img/lowrider/v4/yzs7.jpg)

* Snug the Z stubs using two M5 screws.
* Z stubs should be perpendicular to the lead screw. Tilt/twist causes Z binding.
* After snugging, verify the nut moves easily as you twist it with a little pressure on the metal plate.
* Leave out the cross-tensioner screws — most people will not use these.
* Make sure the Z\_nut is already in place.

---

![YZ back wheel 7](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw7.jpg)

* Assemble the back wheel. Bolt heads face out or down. Snug up the bolts.

---

![YZ back wheel 8](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw8.jpg)

* Pre-thread the nut onto the screw to loosen the lock nut before assembly. More than once helps.
* Slow and easy on this step — make sure not to cross-thread.

---

![YZ back wheel 8a](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw8a.jpg)

* Insert the "hidden" M5 nut. Use a drop of glue if you want to hold the nut while aligning.
* Snug up the wheel assembly with the M5. Make certain it is not cross-threaded.
* Make sure the outer faces of both parts are flush.
* Sloppy wheel alignment leads to the beam not being square to the table. If your finished build
  sits crooked, check here first.

---

![YZ back wheel 8b](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw8b.jpg)

* Make sure the outer faces of both parts are flush.

---

![YZ Z endstop set](/img/lowrider/v4/yzw9.jpg)

* Take the time to set your Z endstops.
* You want them to trigger just before the bearing blocks hit the top stop block, on both sides.
* Measuring the trigger position from a fixed point gets them really close and speeds up Z leveling later.
