# Strut Plates

The strut plates are the first real cuts your LowRider v4 makes — they replace the temporary
printed struts you have been using and give the beam its final rigidity.

## Making the Strut Plates

At this point you should be ready to make some cuts. Refer to
[Basic software setup](https://docs.v1e.com/software/estlcam-basics/) and
[Basic CAM settings](https://docs.v1e.com/tools/milling-basics/) if needed.

The two strut plates should be 6.35 mm (1/4") or thinner MDF or similarly rigid material. No 1/4"
ply — that is too soft. Plastic or metal are fine but harder for a new CNC user to cut.

---

![Strut plates material loaded](/img/lowrider/v4/splates1.jpg)

* Check that your material is relatively square on the machine.
* Secure the material and make sure your endmill will not hit it while passing nearby.

---

![Strut plate file](/img/lowrider/v4/strut.png)

* Use the [calculator](./calculator) and [Vector76 Strut Generator](https://vector76.github.io/Web_OpenSCAD_Customizer/strut_plate.html)
  to make your custom file.
* `strut_length` = value from the [calculator](./calculator)
* `num_braces` = leave at default
* `size of dogbone...` = 3.5 for a 1/8" (3 mm) endmill
* Check both `front_plate` and `bottom_plate`
* `front_wing_size` = 11.5 for 29.5 mm (1" EMT), or your conduit size
* Click `Render (Ctrl+Enter)` → download `strut_plate.svg`

---

![Strut plates in EstlCAM](/img/lowrider/v4/splates2.jpg)

* Open the SVG in EstlCAM using millimeters.
* Rotate the drawing layer 90 degrees.
* Move your zero near the lower left corner — this is where you will position the machine after homing.

---

![Strut plates drill](/img/lowrider/v4/splates3.jpg)

* Beginners should cut one plate at a time.
* Drill all holes first.
* Helical drill gets perfect larger-diameter holes with a smaller endmill.

---

![Strut plates part cut](/img/lowrider/v4/splates4.jpg)

* Use "Part" to cut outside the line.
* Use several "tabs" to keep your part in the material after cutting out.

---

![Strut plates select all](/img/lowrider/v4/splates5.jpg)

* Use the "Select" tool to drag a rectangle around the entire project to select all paths.
* Adjust all settings to match: depth of cut (at least 0.2 mm further than your material),
  finishing path size and tool, tab width and thickness.
* Export your program and cut those parts!

---

## Swapping to Custom Strut Plates

Now to put the new strut plates on your CNC — a second chance to clean up any sloppy wiring.

![Strut plates swap start](/img/lowrider/v4/sr1.jpg)

* Start by removing the X belt and both Y belts.
* Bundle and rubber band them to prevent snagging.

---

![Strut plates remove old](/img/lowrider/v4/sr2.jpg)

* Loosen and remove all front plate X rail screws.
* Remove all four temp strut plates.

---

![Strut plates remove core](/img/lowrider/v4/sr3.jpg)

* To remove the Core, remove the whole side or slide out the top rail enough to slip off the Core.
* You can also slide the Core to the far end and shimmy the strut plates under it.

---

![Strut plates front plate](/img/lowrider/v4/sr4.jpg)

* Add the front strut plate — line up the braces.
* Put the plate straight in; do not wedge it top-to-bottom as that's harder.
* Do not push too hard or you can crack the plate.
* If having issues, pop the two rails out to give more room.
* Add screws loosely; you may need to shimmy things around. Snug later.

---

![Strut plates core back on](/img/lowrider/v4/sr5.jpg)

* Put the core back on.
* Add the last two screws.

---

![Strut plates bottom plate](/img/lowrider/v4/sr6.jpg)

* Add the bottom plate, same as the front.
* Tip: tip in the X min side first to get the edge under the M3 bearing block screws.
* Once all bottom screws and nuts are loosely in, go back and snug them up.

---

![Strut plates board box mount](/img/lowrider/v4/sr7.jpg)

* Use the V1 board box to mark and drill holes in your strut plate.
* M3×10 to secure.

---

![Strut plates heel toe check](/img/lowrider/v4/ht1.jpg)

* Double-check your heel-toe measurements are equal.
* The last two screws on either side of the lower strut plate control your heel-toe distance.
* Measure the YZ Plate, not the front or back wheel mounts.
* Measure across the back of the YZ Plates.

---

![Strut plates heel toe front](/img/lowrider/v4/ht2.jpg)

* Now measure across the front of the YZ Plate.
* The two numbers should be as close as you can measure.
* Adjustment: loosen the 2 or 4 screws on the lower strut plate and shift the plate, then re-snug.
* **Pro-Tip** — if you cannot get these right, the lower X rail is likely too long. The lower X
  rail should not touch the metal XZ plate.

---

![Strut plates snug front](/img/lowrider/v4/sr8.jpg)

* Snug all front plate screws. There should be a visible gap in the brace clamp even when snug.
  These screws just keep the plate snug to the braces.
* Put your X belt back on.

---

![Strut plates Z width check](/img/lowrider/v4/sr9.jpg)

* Check the Z axis: the width across the top and bottom should be the same.
* Adjustment: the front plate 2 screws on each end.

---

## Final Checklist

Now that assembly and adjustments are done:

* Check your Y belt endstop position — sometimes the width changes slightly. If you need to move
  the Min Y belt holder, drill out the screw holes to give yourself more wiggle room.
* Recheck the machine square.
* Recheck the machine Z level.

At this point you are done — you have a fully functional machine! Before you go off into the wild
world of CNC: make test cuts. All included settings and instructions are extremely conservative —
there is no such thing as perfect settings. Have fun and go get that thing dirty.
